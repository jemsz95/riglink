# Brand direction

**Warm and approachable.** Field service software is often cold, dense and
faintly punitive. riglink should feel like a well-organised workshop: warm,
legible, calm, competent. Nothing decorative that costs contrast.

## The three decisions

**Warm neutrals, not cool grey.** Every neutral sits around hue 80 with very
low chroma. It reads as paper and workbench rather than server rack, and it
makes the status colours (which are all chromatic) unambiguous against it.

**Teal-green as primary.** Calm and trustworthy without being corporate blue,
and far enough from every status hue that a primary button is never mistaken
for a state. It is the colour of _actions_.

**Amber for attention only.** Warm amber is the loudest thing in the palette,
so it is rationed: quotes awaiting approval, blocked jobs, a non-empty offline
upload queue. If amber becomes decoration it stops meaning anything, and the
one screen where it matters gets ignored.

## Constraints that outrank aesthetics

1. **Sunlight legibility.** A technician reads this on a scratched phone
   outdoors. Contrast targets are functional requirements, not polish — see the
   ratios on the Colour page.
2. **Gloved hands.** 44px minimum touch targets, 48px in the field shell.
3. **Colour is never the only signal.** Every status carries an icon and a
   text label. Roughly 1 in 12 men has a colour vision deficiency, and this is
   a trades product.
4. **Numbers must not jitter.** Money, quantities and IDs use tabular figures
   so columns stay still as values change.

## Radius and shape

`--radius: 0.75rem`, noticeably rounded. It is the main carrier of
"approachable" and the cheapest one — it costs no contrast.
