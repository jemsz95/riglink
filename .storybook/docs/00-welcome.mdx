# riglink design guide

This is the living visual reference for riglink — a field service management
product with a client portal. It is generated from the same code the app ships,
so it cannot drift: every colour, size and shadow on these pages is read from
the real stylesheet at runtime.

## How to read it

- **Design Tokens** — the raw vocabulary: colour, type, spacing, radius,
  elevation, motion. Start here.
- **Patterns** — how the vocabulary combines: density, status language,
  empty/error/loading states, form conventions.
- **Primitives** — unstyled-to-styled building blocks from shadcn/ui.
- **App** — our own generic components: shells, tables, page headers.
- **Domain** — components that know about jobs, quotes, evidence, invoices.
- **Pages** — full route compositions.

## Two switches, top right

Every story renders under a **theme** (light/dark) and a **density**
(compact/comfortable). Density is not a theme — it is an ergonomics axis:
_compact_ is the office table, _comfortable_ is a phone held in a gloved hand
at a job site. A component must work in all four combinations.

## The audiences

Three surfaces share one design system:

| Surface       | Who                 | Conditions                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Staff app     | Owners, dispatchers | Desktop, dense, keyboard-driven                   |
| Field         | Technicians         | Phone, one-handed, sunlight, gloves, poor signal  |
| Client portal | Customers           | Occasional use, unfamiliar, must feel trustworthy |

When those pull in different directions, the field wins on ergonomics and the
portal wins on clarity. The staff app can afford density because its users are
in it daily.
